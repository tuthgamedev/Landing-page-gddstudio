FAQ

Common questions

Do I need to log in to use the features? +
No account is needed to try the sandbox, explore feature previews, or read and download the public example. Sign in to create and manage GDDs, use AI in your workspace, export, sync to the cloud, or collaborate with a team.
What is a GDD and do I really need one? +
A Game Design Document is the blueprint of your game — it captures mechanics, story, art direction, and scope in one place. You don't need a perfect one to start, but having one saves hours of team miscommunication and scope creep down the road.
I'm a solo dev. Is this overkill for me? +
Not at all. Even a one-page GDD helps you clarify your own thinking and avoid making contradictory design decisions. Our Starter templates are designed to be light and fast — you can have something useful in under an hour.
Can I export and own my GDD? +
Yes. Your document belongs to you. You can export to PDF or Markdown at any time on any plan, including the free tier. We will never lock your content behind a paywall.
What happens if I cancel my plan? +
You move to the Starter tier. You keep access to all your existing GDDs in read mode and can export them. You won't lose anything you've written.
Is there a template for [my genre]? +
We launch with 12 genre templates covering RPG, Platformer, Puzzle, FPS/TPS, Roguelike, Visual Novel, Strategy, Simulation, Fighting, Horror, Sports, and a Blank canvas. We add templates based on community requests.

Complete GDD example

Fictional example. No login needed to read this document.

Ember Quest

Genre: 2D adventure RPG · Platform: PC · Session: about 10 minutes

01 · Game concept

The player is a forest keeper collecting embers to relight the village's lanterns. Short exploration sessions and light puzzles are the focus.

02 · Rules and gameplay loop

Explore, collect embers, light a lantern, and unlock the next path. Lighting a lantern consumes embers. Win by lighting three lanterns and returning to the village.

03 · World and story

A storm has left the village without light. Follow the old keeper's trail and help the villagers find their way home through the forest.

04 · Art direction

2D pixel art with muted forest colors and warm orange lanterns. Interactive objects use clear shapes and markers, not color alone.

05 · Music and sound

Quiet ambient music, footsteps on leaves, and short cues when an ember is collected or a lantern lights up.

06 · First-version scope

One forest area, three puzzles, one enemy type, and one ending. Multiplayer, crafting, and an open world are excluded from the first version.
